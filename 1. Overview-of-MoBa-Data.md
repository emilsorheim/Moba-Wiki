# Overview of MoBa Data

## Scope and Purpose

The Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study (MoBa) is a longitudinal, population-based cohort study conducted by the Norwegian Institute of Public Health (FHI). MoBa is a prospective cohort study in which participants are enrolled during pregnancy and followed over time. The cohort comprises mothers recruited during pregnancy, fathers of the participating pregnancies and children followed from birth onwards. This structure enables analyses at the individual, family, and intergenerational level. 

The study was established to generate knowledge about the causes, risk factors, courses, and consequences of diseases and health outcomes, with the objective of improving prevention and treatment. This objective determines both the design of the cohort and the structure of the data. The data support analyses of associations between exposures and health outcomes, temporal development of disease, interactions between genetic and environmental factors and variation in risk across individuals and families  

---

## Types of Data in MoBa

MoBa integrates multiple complementary data sources:

### 1. Questionnaire Data
- Collected from mothers, fathers, and children  
- Covers:
  - Physical and mental health  
  - Lifestyle and diet  
  - Socioeconomic and psychosocial factors  
- Repeated across time (pregnancy → adulthood)

---

### 2. Biological Data (Biobank)
- Blood, urine, cord blood, and other samples  
- Enables:
  - Genetic analyses (GWAS)  
  - Biomarker research  
  - Omics data (e.g., epigenetics, metabolomics)
---

### Registry Linkages
MoBa can be linked to national health registries, which allows comprehensive follow-up across the life course. This provides:

- Diagnoses and hospital data  
- Prescription data  
- Mortality and long-term outcomes  

This is only granted through a formal application process, and projects must fullfil a set of requirements before access to data is provided. Further details can be found here: [Slik søker du om MoBa-data (FHI)](https://www.fhi.no/op/studier/moba/forskere/forskning-og-datatilgang-fra-den-no/)
