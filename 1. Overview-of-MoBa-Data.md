# Overview of MoBa Data

## What is MoBa?

The Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study (MoBa) is a large, population-based longitudinal cohort study conducted by the Norwegian Institute of Public Health (FHI).

- ~114,000 children  
- ~95,000 mothers  
- ~75,000 fathers  
- Recruitment during pregnancy (1999–2008)  
- Ongoing follow-up across the life course  

MoBa is one of the largest family-based cohort studies in the world and includes both questionnaire data and biological samples.

---

## Scientific Purpose of MoBa

The core purpose of MoBa is:

> To generate new knowledge about the **causes, risk factors, courses, and consequences of diseases and health outcomes**, with the ultimate goal of improving prevention and treatment.

This includes:

- Identifying **causal relationships** between exposures and health outcomes  
- Studying how **genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors interact**  
- Understanding how health develops **from fetal life and throughout the life course**  
- Providing a scientific basis for **disease prevention and public health interventions**

## Why MoBa is Designed This Way

MoBa is a **prospective cohort study**, meaning:

- Data on exposures are collected **before disease develops**
- This enables more reliable identification of **causal factors**

The study specifically focuses on:

- Pregnancy and early life as critical periods for later health  
- Long-term follow-up to understand disease trajectories  
- Family-based data (mother–father–child) to study:
  - genetic effects  
  - environmental exposures  
  - intergenerational influences

## Types of Data in MoBa

MoBa integrates multiple complementary data sources:

### 1. Questionnaire Data
- Collected from mothers, fathers, and children  
- Covers:
  - Physical and mental health  
  - Lifestyle and diet  
  - Socioeconomic and psychosocial factors  
- Repeated across time (pregnancy → adulthood)

---

### 2. Biological Data (Biobank)
- Blood, urine, cord blood, and other samples  
- Enables:
  - Genetic analyses (GWAS)  
  - Biomarker research  
  - Omics data (e.g., epigenetics, metabolomics)

---

### 3. Registry Linkages
MoBa can be linked to national health registries, providing:

- Diagnoses and hospital data  
- Prescription data  
- Mortality and long-term outcomes  

This allows comprehensive follow-up across the life course.

---

## Key Strengths of MoBa

- **Large sample size** enabling study of rare exposures and outcomes  
- **Longitudinal design** with repeated measurements  
- **Family-based structure** (mother–father–child triads)  
- **Rich data integration** (questionnaire + biological + registry data)  
- **Life-course perspective** from fetal life into adulthood  

---

## What This Means for Researchers

MoBa enables:

- Epidemiological studies of disease etiology  
- Gene–environment interaction analyses  
- Longitudinal modeling of health trajectories  
- Validation of biomarkers and risk factors  

However, the complexity of the data requires:

- Understanding of data structure and identifiers  
- Careful handling of variable coding and versions  
- Awareness of data limitations
